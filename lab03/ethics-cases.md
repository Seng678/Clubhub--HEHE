## Scenario A: "Just a small report"

| Step | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| 1 Facts | The Robotics Club leader asks for a report, visible to club leaders, showing which students attended other clubs' events, "so we can recruit the active ones". The leader is friends with the clubs office and says the administrator "won't mind". Club Hub collects attendance only to run events and the monthly activity report. Students were never told it would be used for recruiting. **Still to find out:** has the administrator actually approved this? Do students know or expect it? Would a numbers-only report meet the club's need? |
| 2 Stakeholders and harms | **Students:** tracked across clubs without consent, possible unwanted recruitment pressure, and attendance can reveal personal interests. **Other club leaders:** may feel their members are being targeted. **Clubs office (data controller):** carries the legal and reputational risk if data is used beyond its purpose. **Robotics Club leader:** wants members, but may face complaints. **Our team:** we would build it, so we share the responsibility. |
| 3 Codes, law, policy | **SE Code Principle 1 (Public):** protect the public interest and privacy. **SE Code Principle 2 (Client and Employer):** our client is the clubs office, not one club leader. **ACM Code 1.6:** respect privacy. **ACM Code 1.2:** avoid harm. **GDPR Art. 5(1)(b), purpose limitation:** attendance was collected for attendance, not recruiting. **GDPR Art. 5(1)(c), data minimisation:** a named list is more data than recruiting needs. |
| 4 Options | (a) Build the report as asked, because the leader says the administrator agrees. (b) Refuse and say nothing more. (c) Decline the named-student report, ask the administrator (the data controller) to decide, and offer alternatives: an attendance count per event with no names, or an opt-in "contact me about other clubs" setting that students choose themselves. (d) Build a named report, but only for students who have opted in. |
| 5 Tests | **(a)** Harm: fails, students are exposed without knowing. Rights: fails, no consent and a new purpose. Fairness: fails, one club gets an advantage from data others cannot use. Publicity: fails, students would object if it appeared in the news. **(b)** Harm, rights, fairness, publicity: all pass, but it offers the leader no help. **(c)** Harm, rights, fairness, publicity: all pass. It protects students, respects the controller's authority and still helps the club. **(d)** Passes if the opt-in is separate, specific and defaults to "no", but it needs the administrator's approval and an updated privacy notice first. |
| 6 Decision and action | Choose **(c)**. Option **(d)** is possible later, if the administrator approves and the privacy notice is updated first. Actions: (1) send the message below to the club leader; (2) tell the Product Owner (Sinath Naihouy) so the request goes to the clubs office; (3) record in our data inventory that recruiting is not an approved purpose; (4) open the preventive issue below. |
| 7 Reflection | A friendly request from someone with influence is still a request to change what the data is used for. The decision belongs to the data controller and the students, not to whoever asks politely. Writing down the purpose of every field makes this kind of request easy to answer: if the purpose is not on the list, the answer starts at "no". |

**Message sent (to the Robotics Club leader):**
> Hi Robotics Club leader, thanks for the idea. We can't build the report that shows which students attended other clubs' events. Students gave us their details to register for events, not to be targeted for recruitment by other clubs, and we never told them this would happen. Building it without their consent would break our own privacy notice. We also can't speak for the clubs office, so we've asked the administrator to confirm. What we can offer: a "Join us" option where students choose to hear from clubs, and an attendance count (numbers only, no names) for your own events. Would either of those help your recruiting? — Setha, Team HEHE

**Preventive change:** Add a role restriction so club leaders only see attendance for their own club's events, with a test that a leader of club X is denied access to club Y's data. GitHub issue #4, label `ethics`.

## Scenario B: "Don't mention it in the demo"

| Step | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| 1 Facts | Two days before the Sprint 1 review, a developer found two defects: (1) cancellations inside the 24-hour window are accepted, which breaks a business rule; (2) the monthly report counts cancelled registrations as attendance, so its numbers are wrong. The Scrum Master says: "Don't mention it in the demo, we'll fix it in Sprint 2. The client will never notice." **Still to find out:** how many registrations are affected, whether any decision has already been based on the report, and whether both fixes with tests fit in two days. |
| 2 Stakeholders and harms | **Client / clubs office:** may make decisions on wrong numbers and loses trust if the concealment is found later. **Students:** may be allowed to cancel when they should not, or wrongly counted as having attended. **Organisers and club leaders:** plan with inaccurate numbers. **The developer:** pressured to stay silent against professional integrity. **Scrum Master and team:** credibility is at risk if the concealment comes out. |
| 3 Codes, law, policy | **SE Code Principle 3 (Product):** be honest about known defects in what we deliver. **SE Code 6.12:** raise concerns through the proper channels, here the Scrum Master first and then the Product Owner. **ACM Code 1.3:** be honest and trustworthy. **ACM Code 2.5:** give a thorough evaluation of the system, including its risks. **GDPR Art. 5(1)(d), accuracy:** the report holds wrong data about people. |
| 4 Options | (a) Stay silent as asked and fix in Sprint 2. (b) Postpone the whole demo until everything is fixed. (c) Demo as planned and list both defects under "Known issues" with a fix date. (d) Fix both defects in the two days, with regression tests, and drop another item from the demo if time is short. |
| 5 Tests | **(a)** Harm: wrong reports may be used. Rights: the client has a right to accurate information about the product. Fairness: fails, the team knows something the client does not. Publicity: fails, the client would feel deceived if it came out later. **(b)** Harm: low. Fairness and publicity: pass, but it wastes the review and protects nothing extra. **(c)** Passes all four tests: it is honest and keeps the schedule. **(d)** Passes all four tests, but only if the fix and its tests fit in the time available. |
| 6 Decision and action | Choose **(c)**, and **(d)** if the regression tests are green before the review. Actions: (1) the developer raises it with the Scrum Master (Setha) today (SE Code 6.12); (2) if the Scrum Master still declines, the Product Owner (Sinath Naihouy) is told and puts it on the review agenda; (3) write failing tests for both defects; (4) list both under "Known issues" with the fix date; (5) correct any report already produced. |
| 7 Reflection | "The client will never notice" is a bet on not being caught, not a reason it is right. Admitting a defect early is cheap, and a client who hears it from us stays on our side. Our review template should have a standing "Known issues" section so that disclosure is routine, not an awkward confession. |

**Message sent (to the Scrum Master and Product Owner):**
> Hi Setha and Sinath, before the Sprint 1 review I found two defects: cancellations inside the 24-hour window are accepted, and the monthly report counts cancelled registrations as attendance. I don't think we should hide this. The client may rely on those numbers, and staying quiet would mislead them. Proposal: (1) I add failing regression tests today and try to fix both by Thursday; (2) either way, we list the defects under "Known issues" in the demo with the fix date. Can we agree this at today's stand-up? I'm happy to present it myself. — Phou Sekang

**Preventive change:** Add a "Known issues" section to the sprint review template, plus regression tests for the 24-hour cancellation rule and for the monthly report excluding cancelled registrations. GitHub issue #5, label `ethics`.
